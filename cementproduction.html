Cement Production

Priority Level: ____

Cement production produces rougly 7% of the world's annual carbon emissions. It does this in two main ways: calcination (~50% of cement production's carbon emissions)and powering cement kilns (~40% of cement production's carbon emissions). These are the two biggest sources of carbon emissions in the cement production process, but a few minor sources include the transportation of materials as well as the grinding of others, which accounts for about 10% of cement production's carbon emissions. Calcination is the chemical process of converting limestone (calcium carbonate, CaCO₃) into lime (calcium oxide, CaO), which is a key component of cement. During this process, CO₂ is released as a byproduct. Cement kilns are primarily used to heat up limestone to extreme temperatures in order for calcination to occur. But the powering of the kilns themselves also emits CO₂, as they are typically powered by fossil fuels.

How can we reduce emissions from cement production?

Cement production has a somewhat sizable impact on global carbon emissions. This makes it an important topic to give attention to, especially considering that there are so many ways in which we can reduce its emissions given our current technology. Let us first turn our attention to the calcination process, as it has the greatest impact on cement productions carbon emissions. In this endeavour there are two main ways to decrease emissions, which can theoretically be combined for maximum emissions mitigation. The first is to look for cement alternatives that do not use lime, so that the calcination process can be avoided entirely. Some of these alternatives have already been discovered and are being researched, such as Geopolymer cement and Magnesium-based cement. The other way to reduce the carbon footprint of calcination is to directly capture the CO₂ as it is produced, and then store it for long term or use it for things such as fuel. A pressing project is one that would analyze the pros and cons of both of these methods and see which one (or what combination of both) is best suited to reduce the carbon footprint of the calcination process. The second part of cement production whose carbon emissions can be reduced is the powering of the kilns. These emissions can be reduced either by powering the kilns with clean energy, or by reducing limestone to lime via direct electrochemical reduction. To recap, strategies to implement to reduce carbon emissions from the cement production process include implementing alternatives to regular cement that do not use lime, using direct carbon capture and storage on calcination emissions, powering kilns with clean energy, and reducing limestone to lime via direct electrochemical reduction.
